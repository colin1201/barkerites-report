What to do with this

1
Pick your sprints — this role runs you harder than the back does.
You covered 100.4 m/min against 61–63 in your last-man games: about 62% more running per minute. 24 sprints in 39 minutes is 6.2 every 10 min. That is the job, not a fault — but it sits near the ceiling of what anyone repeats for a full game, so which runs you make is the whole thing.
2
Warm up to near-max — or your first sprint of the game is your warm-up.
You hit 28.3 km/h, 96% of your flat-out best. The quickest thing in your current warm-up is a 70–80% stride, so the game took you somewhere your preparation never does. Reaching 96% cold, late on, is the classic way a hamstring goes. One near-max stride before push-back covers it.
3
Look at where your sprints bunched — the map knows where, you know why.
16 of your 24 sprints came in 6 bunches less than a minute apart, the longest 4 back-to-back, typically 48 s between efforts. Bunching is exactly what pressing looks like, so this is not a fault — it is a question: were those the press, or chasing balls that were gone? You will remember.
